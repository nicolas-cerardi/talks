  • About 50 000 satellite trajectories to simulate...
  • Instead, we can model the density of satellite in the sky with an analytical model from [Bassa, Hainaut & Galadi-Enriquez, 2022].
  • We adapted this model and applied it to the SKAO in [Cerardi, Tolley, di Vruno, 2026].
  • So far, a geometrical prediction only (no forecast of the received power).

The exposition of the SKA-Low to mega constellations



  • Significantly exposed to satellites at all frequencies, up to 100% below 100 MHz.
  • Flagging will not be a viable mitigation, other techniques must be developed, e.g. RFI subtraction (see Chris Finlay's Tabascal).
  • More predictions for SKA-Low and Mid in [Cerardi, Tolley, di Vruno, 2026].

  • Use the analytical density to spawn satellites at the FoV edges.

Spectral properties of the satellite sample

[Di Vruno+23]
  • Focus on the 157 - 165 MHz band, 100 kHz resolution.

Power spectrum of the RFI component

  • Excess power located at small $ k_{\perp} $ and all $ k_{\parallel} $.
  • Strong line at high $ k_{\parallel} $, possibly from the 200kHz oscillation.
  • RFI power plateaus at low $ k $.
  • Overall RFI power decreases with longer observation.

Conclusions and future work

Take aways
Forecasts of the SKAO RFI environment with analytical modelling of satellite constellations
  • Prediction of the number/rate of satellites crossing the MB. Up to 100% at low frequencies.
  • Estimation of the power spectrum contribution from faint UEMR. Strongest RFI power is found near the EoR window, but decreases with observing time.
Future work:
  • Simulations for longer exposures and diverse spectral features.
  • Comparison with noise power.
